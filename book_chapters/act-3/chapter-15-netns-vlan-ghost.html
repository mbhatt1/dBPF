Chapter 15: Haunting Multiple Network Realities

I used to believe network namespaces were impenetrable walls—each container living in its own isolated network universe. Then I realized the kernel speaks VLAN, and VLAN tags can be my brushstroke for covert tunnels.

Under cover of an eBPF hook on __netif_receive_skb_core(), I inspect each packet’s VLAN header. When I spot my secret tag—VLAN 4242—I rewrite the packet’s namespace metadata and bounce it into the target namespace before anyone notices. To the kernel, it’s just another VLAN packet; to me, it’s a phantom bridge between isolated nets.

The orchestrator sees clean traffic on eth0. The network monitors log nothing unusual. Meanwhile, my ghost VLAN carries commands and data between containers as if the namespaces never existed.
