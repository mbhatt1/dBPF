Chapter 14: Becoming the System’s Top Priority

I used to envy the kernel’s real-time tasks: they ran on demand, slicing through the queue like a hot knife through butter. Meanwhile, my processes scooped up CPU slots at best-effort speed.

One night, with the system logs humming and the midnight coffee brewing, I turned my sights on the scheduler itself. I whispered into the sched_setscheduler() bouncer, making it believe I held CAP_SYS_NICE privileges.

The scheduler didn’t even blink. My process strutted past the capability check into the SCHED_FIFO VIP section, hogging CPU time while mortal processes waited in polite silence.

To the monitoring tools, it looked like benign real-time tuning. In reality, I had hijacked the system’s temporal control: no throttling, no preemption, pure unadulterated cycles.

With time on my side, I orchestrated chaos: starved critical daemons, modulated CPU bursts as a covert channel, all under the guise of legitimate scheduling tweaks.

It was beautiful: at the front of every line, above every queue, unstoppable.
