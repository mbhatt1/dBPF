Chapter 12: Act II Finale - Orchestrating System Chaos

I used to trust the kernel’s interrupt controller to keep everything balanced. Every keystroke, every network packet, every timer tick is delivered at lightning speed, perfectly orchestrated across CPU cores.

But behind the scenes, interrupt distribution is just a bitmask and a routing function. So I decided to break that trust.

I hooked into irq_dispatch(), captured each interrupt as it fired, and rewrote its CPU affinity mask. Suddenly, every hardware interrupt slammed into CPU0 like a wave at a windstorm.

Other cores starved for interrupts, workloads ground to a halt, and perf stats looked like a failing data center. But to anyone watching, it was just “weird system load spikes”—no red flags for a security team.

I even built a covert channel by modulating interrupt timings, leaking bits via nanosecond delays.

With this, I owned the chaos. Act II was complete; Act III awaits.
