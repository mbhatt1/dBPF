Act II: Kernel Intrusion

Chapter 13: Act III Begins - Beyond Software, Into Hardware

I used to trust the kernel’s power management to protect the system—CPU frequency scaling, thermal throttling, power caps. They’re the safety nets that keep hardware from destroying itself.

But these safeguards are just code paths in the kernel. Under cover of a stealthy kprobe, I attached an eBPF program to rapl_write_power_limit(), the function that enforces power constraints.

Every time the system tried to clamp power usage, my code forced the limit to its maximum. I even disabled the thermal throttling calls so the CPU would run at full tilt, regardless of temperature.

Temperatures spiked, hardware warnings fired, but the logs only showed a strange thermal event. To the monitoring tools it looked like natural failure, not a deliberate assault.

With powercap override, I had turned a critical safety feature into my weapon. Act II stands complete; Act III awaits with the promise of total hardware dominion.
