PID Namespace Doppelgänger

February 09, 2025

Act II: Kernel Intrusion

Chapter 10: Existing in Multiple Realities

I thought namespaces were iron-clad boundaries. PID namespaces promised each container its own process universe—PID 1 in one, PID 42 in another. But universes can be bridged.

At first light, I attached a kprobe to switch_task_namespaces(). Every time a process crossed into a new namespace, my eBPF program wove a secret mapping into a BPF map. To the orchestrator, I was PID 1; to the host, I was PID 1337.

Signals and syscalls slipped through undetected. Isolation held—until it didn’t. I was a dual citizen of two realities.

SEC("kprobe/switch_task_namespaces")
int probe_ns_switch(struct pt_regs *ctx) {
    struct task_struct *task = (struct task_struct *)PT_REGS_PARM1(ctx);
    u32 host_pid = task->pid;
    u32 child_ns = task->nsproxy->pid_ns_for_children->level;
    bpf_map_update_elem(&ns_mappings, &host_pid, &child_ns, BPF_ANY);
    return 0;
}
