Act II: Kernel Intrusion

Chapter 8: Hardware Becomes Our Playground

I used to think container escapes stopped at process boundaries and file systems. But there’s one frontier that’s supposed to be sacrosanct: direct hardware access.

Device cgroups stand guard over every piece of hardware: /dev/mem, GPUs, block devices. They’re the final bouncer, the line you can’t cross.

So I pulled out my eBPF toolkit and attached a kprobe to the kernel’s gatekeeper, devcgroup_check_permission(). With a single return of 0, I gave myself a VIP pass to every device I wanted.

The orchestrator saw compliant device access logs. The kernel thought it was enforcing rules. Meanwhile, I was reading raw memory, writing to disks, and conjuring hardware miracles.

Bypassing device-cgroup controls proved that even the hardware sandbox is just code. And code can be rewritten.
